Guide

How to choose ERP software

Choosing an ERP is easier when you start from your own workflow instead of a feature list. This guide gives you a fair set of criteria, including ones a vendor might not volunteer.

1. Start with a workflow, not a feature list

Feature lists make every product look similar. Pick the one workflow that costs you the most time, for example taking an order and getting it delivered and paid, and ask each vendor to show it from start to finish. You will learn more in twenty minutes than from a month of brochures.

2. Write down your exceptions

Every business has a normal path and a list of things that go wrong. Write yours down: partial deliveries, discounts outside policy, customers over their credit limit, goods that arrive damaged, returns after payment. A good system has a clear answer for each. A weak one only works when nothing goes wrong.

3. Ask what is recorded at each step

For every stage of the workflow, ask what is captured, who is responsible and what record results. Pay particular attention to the difference between events that are often confused: goods received and supplier bill received, order delivered and order invoiced, invoice raised and payment received. Systems that treat these as one thing hide problems.

4. Understand the accounting boundary

Some products include full accounting. Others handle operations and work alongside your existing accounting software. Either can be right. What matters is that you know which system holds the official financial records, how documents flow between the two, and what happens when they disagree. Be wary of vague wording such as basic accounting.

5. Ask about tax and statutory coverage

GST, e-invoicing and similar requirements change over time and differ from business to business. Ask exactly which functions are covered, how updates are handled and who confirms the result. Confirm with your accountant. Be cautious of any vendor who promises full compliance in general terms.

6. Look at growth

You may be a ten-person business today and thirty tomorrow. Ask whether you can add users, locations, approvals and specialist workflows without moving to a different product, and whether your data and history stay with you.

7. Ask about your data

Can you import your existing customers, suppliers, products and opening stock? Can you export your records in a usable form if you leave? What happens to your data after you cancel?

8. Ask what is not included

Every product has limits. Ask what is outside the core, what costs extra, and what is a separate service, such as data cleaning, training or custom development. A vendor who answers plainly is usually a vendor who will be straightforward later.

9. Run the demo on your own cases

Bring a real order, a real purchase and a real awkward case. See whether the product handles them or needs a workaround.

Questions to take to every demo

  • Can you show our busiest workflow from start to finish?
  • What happens when part of an order is delivered?
  • How do we trace why a stock quantity changed?
  • Which system holds the official accounts, and how do the two connect?
  • What can we change ourselves, and what needs your help?
  • How do we get our data out?
