Journey 1

Order to cash

From a customer’s request to money received. Follow a customer’s demand from the first record through delivery, invoicing and collection.

    A customer asks for goods or services.

    RecordedCustomerItemsQuantitiesRequested price

  2. Check

    Customer, items, price, discount and credit position are checked where supported.

    RecordedWarnings or approvals

  3. Confirm

    The order becomes a commitment.

  4. Check stock and arrange fulfilment

    Availability is checked; goods are picked, or a purchase is raised.

    RecordedStock positionReservations where supported

  5. Dispatch or deliver

    Goods leave.

    RecordedDelivered quantities against the order

  6. Invoice

    An invoice is created or recorded.

    RecordedInvoice linked to the order

  7. Collect

    A payment or receipt is recorded and allocated.

    RecordedAmount received against the invoice

  8. Follow up

    Pending orders, overdue balances and returns are monitored.

  9. Reconcile

    Sales, stock and money are compared.

    RecordedDifferences surfaced for review

Three statuses, not one.

An order can be delivered but unpaid, invoiced but not yet delivered, or partly delivered and partly invoiced. So OneSync tracks the following separately instead of forcing everything into a single status.

Fulfilment

Not started, partly fulfilled, fulfilled

Invoicing

Not invoiced, partly invoiced, invoiced

Payment

Unpaid, partly paid, settled

Journey 2

Purchase to payment

From a need to buy to a supplier paid. Track what the business needs to buy, what was ordered, what arrived, what was accepted and what remains payable.

  1. Identify the need

    Low stock, a customer order or a request creates a purchase need.

  2. Choose a supplier

    Price and terms are checked against supplier records.

  3. Approve

    An approval is recorded where the business requires it.

  4. Order

    A purchase order or agreed purchase record is created.

  5. Receive

    Actual received quantities are recorded, separate from the ordered quantities.

  6. Resolve differences

    Short, excess, damaged or rejected quantities are handled.

  7. Record the supplier bill

    The bill is recorded.

  8. Match

    Bill, order and receipt are compared where the business needs it.

  9. Pay

    Payment is recorded and the supplier balance updates.

Four events, kept separate.

Goods received, supplier bill received, amount owed and payment made are four different events. A receipt can add stock before the bill arrives, and a bill can arrive before the goods. OneSync keeps them as separate steps, linked together, so that each one can be seen and checked.

Situations to be ready for: a rejected request, a supplier changing price or delivery date, a receipt larger or smaller than ordered, goods that fail inspection, a duplicate supplier bill, a bill that disagrees with the order, a supplier credit note instead of a refund, a delayed payment record, and a correction after posting.

How stock fits in

Every change in stock has a reason.

In OneSync, stock quantities change only through recorded events. Each event leaves a movement that can be traced back to the document or person behind it. There is no way to quietly overwrite a balance.

That is why you can ask “why is this item’s stock 80?” and trace it, and why a physical count that disagrees leads to a reviewed adjustment instead of a silent edit.

{{icon:history}}

Events that can change stock

  • Opening stock brought in when you start
  • Goods received against a purchase
  • Goods dispatched against an order
  • Transfers between locations, where you use more than one
  • Reservations and releases, where supported
  • Counts and adjustments, with a reason and a responsible user
  • Returns, damage, quarantine or disposal, where supported
{{icon:layers}}

Different quantities, kept apart

A good stock record separates what is physically on hand from what is reserved, in transit or held back. OneSync keeps physical quantity separate from other quantity states where the product supports them.

{{icon:shield}}

Control principles

  • Every stock movement can be traced to its source document or an authorised adjustment.
  • Available quantity is calculated one agreed way, not edited separately in different places.
  • A repeated delivery or receipt request does not create duplicate movements.
  • Corrections keep history instead of erasing it.
  • Negative stock, overselling and back-dated entries follow explicit rules, not accidents.

Returns and corrections

A return is more than a minus sign.

When goods come back, several things may be true at once. The stock may return to saleable condition, wait for inspection, or be written off. The customer may receive a refund, a credit or a replacement. An invoice may need a correction. OneSync treats a return as its own workflow, linked to the original sale, instead of a negative quantity typed against stock.

{{icon:undo}}

Outcomes a return can have

  • Goods back into saleable stock
  • Goods into quarantine pending inspection
  • Damaged or scrapped goods
  • Exchange or replacement
  • Credit without return, where policy allows
  • Refund paid or pending
  • Supplier replacement, credit or refund
  • Invoice correction, credit or debit note, where applicable
  • A transaction cancelled before any downstream effect
